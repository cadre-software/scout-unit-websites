Set up your unit website

A Pack or Troop website with your information, photos, and a working way for families to get in touch.

  1. Enter your unit information

    Open Make it yours. Choose Pack or Troop, replace the example meeting details and costs, and add your actual application, council, and public calendar links. Under Page sections, choose each part of the page to edit its headings, descriptions, buttons and images.

  2. Choose your images

    Select permitted photos and a Scouting emblem from the library, or upload your unit’s own images. Describe an uploaded activity photo for visitors who cannot see it.

  3. Connect the contact form

    The website displays the form; a hosted form service delivers the message to your adult membership team. You do not need to run a mail server.

    For example, with Formspree:

    1. Create a unit-owned account and verify its email address.
    2. Create a new form. In its dashboard, configure and verify the recipient address. Use an adult role inbox, such as membership@example.org.
    3. Copy the public form endpoint from the form’s Integration section. It looks like https://formspree.io/f/your-form-id.
    4. Paste it into Form service address in this editor. The backup contact email field does not configure delivery.
    5. Enable the provider’s spam protection. If you restrict submissions to your website’s domain, include the address where you will publish.

    Several recipients: configure additional verified recipients in the provider’s dashboard if your plan supports it, or use a unit-owned group address that distributes messages to the adult membership team. Confirm that the group accepts outside messages and delivers to each intended member.

    Keep recipient access current when volunteers change. Private mail passwords and API keys belong in the provider’s account, never in this editor or the downloaded website.

    The form uses standard HTML POST fields: name, email, message, subject, and an empty _gotcha spam trap. Other hosted services can work if they accept those fields at a public HTTPS endpoint.

  4. Save your settings and download the website

    Choose Save settings and keep the JSON file with your unit’s website records. It includes your entered details, library selections, and uploaded images. Load it in the editor whenever you need an update. Then review the publishing checklist and choose Download website to save index.html.

    The settings file is for the editor; the index.html file is the website. The downloaded website contains no editor. Keep the editor and settings file on your own computer if you prefer: neither needs to be published with your website. Protect the hosting account with a unique password and two-step sign-in. The editor has no server-side saving or publishing access, so visitors cannot use it to change the hosted site. The exported page includes its displayed images, fonts, and navigation. You can review it from disk, but publish it at a web address to test the contact form.

  5. Publish and test

    Have your website helper publish index.html on your chosen static host and connect your domain. A WordPress or Squarespace site can use its own native form instead; this download is not a native theme for those builders.

    Send a short test through the form on the published site. Confirm the provider’s confirmation page appears, the message arrives in every intended inbox, and Reply addresses the person who submitted it. Check spam folders and the service’s submission limits. A confirmation page means the service accepted the submission; inbox delivery must be checked separately.

    Class examples and the editor preview do not send messages. Use only adult contact details and keep health records and sensitive information out of a general inquiry.
